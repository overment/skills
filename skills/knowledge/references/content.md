# Content

Open this when deciding what goes into the base and how it's written. This is the base's content bar: put a short version of it, in the base's own words, into the context file.

## What earns a place

- **What can't be regenerated:** decisions and their reasons, local facts and numbers, what a source actually says, what was tried and failed, the person's own views. General knowledge gets a line or a link, not a note, unless learning it is the base's purpose; then the note is the reader's own restatement, linked to what it connects to. Where the base's view differs from the general one, store the difference.
- **Distilled, not dumped:** transcripts, clippings and exports are sources. Keep them in `archive/`, under names that can't collide (date plus original name), and write the notes they justify.
- **Worth a future question:** if no survey question would lead to it, it belongs somewhere else, such as a task list or a calendar, or nowhere.

## Understand before storing

- Restate it in a sentence or two. If you can't, you don't understand it yet: read more, ask, or store it as an open question (Feynman technique).
- Search before creating. Extend the note that already covers it, because a near-duplicate is worse than no note.
- Link it to what it supports, contradicts or replaces, and say which. Never leave two contradicting notes side by side without a mark; mark the superseded fact and link it to the newer one.
- Keep provenance. Separate what a source says from what you conclude, and date anything that can go stale. Inside a verbatim quote, add the absolute date in brackets rather than rewriting the words.

## Writing

Rules 1 and 7 hold for every note. Compress the words, never the reasoning: each claim keeps its reason, because the reason lets a reader apply it to a case the note didn't foresee.

A raw capture:

> mtg w/ Jonas re onboarding — thinks 3-day workshop too long (day 3 repeats day 2), maybe 2? also week-1 dropout was high, look at it

It holds two things that change for different reasons, so it becomes a note and a task:

> **Onboarding workshop: two days instead of three (proposed)** · `type: decision` · `status: proposed` · `date: 2026-06-03`
>
> Jonas proposes cutting the onboarding workshop from three days to two, because day three repeats day two. Source: meeting with Jonas, 2026-06-03.

> Task: look into the high week-one dropout (from the meeting with Jonas, 2026-06-03).

## Intake

Choose how new material enters:

- **Directly into place,** when writers are agents with the context file loaded.
- **Through an inbox,** when capture must be instant. Say who empties it and when.
- **From the conversation,** when the person states a fact while asking a question. File it the same way, citing the conversation and its date as the source.
- **From automated feeds,** where a script does the mechanical part and each input is filed once ([scale](scale.md)).
